# Calibration, tiers, and anti-patterns

Load this reference after deciding that meme injection is appropriate and you need to calibrate a specific register, language, or freshness tier.

## Stability tiers

| Tier | Typical age | Risk of aging | Use |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Stable** | 10+ years | low | safest default across registers |
| **In rotation** | 1–3 years | moderate | use when currentness helps the piece |
| **Risky / platform-coded** | <1 year | high | use only when dating the prose to the moment is acceptable |

Tier is a hint, not a verdict. A current-format post may want a risky meme; an evergreen essay usually should not. When presenting candidates, disclose the tier when it materially affects the choice.

## Register calibration

| Register | Inline tendencies | Block tendencies | Usually avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dry observational essay | structural forms, X-coded, duality | escalation/list structures | performative stan register |
| Personal/confessional | self-aware inline forms | dialogic / "they don't know" | 4chan-coded greentext |
| Cultural criticism | performative/structural mix | dialogic blocks | stale generic irony |
| Weird/cosmic philosophy | deadpan structural, absurdist | greentext, escalation | TikTok-heavy register unless intentional |
| Tech/dev blog | dry structural, "this is fine" | greentext | pop-stan vocabulary |
| Comedic review | broad palette | dialogic/POV/alignment | over-explaining the joke |

If the register is genuinely ambiguous, clarify before injecting humor into sensitive or formally serious prose.

## Mixed-language posts

- Keep a meme in the language of the surrounding sentence unless code-switching is itself the joke.
- Do not translate internet phrases literally when the translated phrase is not idiomatic.
- Loanwords can survive across PT/EN when they already function naturally in the target register.
- Treat a deliberate language switch as a marked rhetorical move, not decoration.

## Anti-patterns

Avoid:

1. stacking multiple inline memes in one sentence;
2. block memes without setup and landing;
3. multiple block memes when one already carries the set-piece function;
4. explaining the meme after using it;
5. deploying aged formats as if they were current;
6. importing stan/TikTok register into serious prose without an intentional register clash;
7. carrying slur/ironic-bigotry baggage from source communities into a clean format such as greentext;
8. forcing a meme into a slot that fails the cut test;
9. humor that punches down or relies on stereotypes about marginalized groups, body shape, class, or mental state;
10. self-harm/dissociation humor without strong contextual reason and user signal;
11. treating risky-tier memes as evergreen;
12. literal translation of untranslatable meme phrases.

## Catalog boundary

The active meme inventories live in:

- [`catalog-en.md`](catalog-en.md)
- [`catalog-pt-br.md`](catalog-pt-br.md)

Use those for concrete forms. This file governs selection quality, not the catalog itself.

Some formats are intentionally weak defaults even if culturally recognizable: highly stan-coded catchphrases, mental-illness self-deprecation, manosphere-coded formats, and visual-only formats that belong in `meme-image`.

## Maintenance

Catalog tiers age. Periodically:

- promote forms that stabilized across registers;
- demote or remove forms that became dated noise;
- add genuinely active new forms cautiously;
- preserve short notes when a tier shift reflects a meaningful editorial decision.

Do not turn maintenance into trend-chasing. The purpose is durable register control.
