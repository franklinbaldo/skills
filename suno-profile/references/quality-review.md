# Quality review: finding and trashing low-quality songs

Part of ongoing curation, not a one-off task — periodically sweep the
catalog for songs worth removing (garbled/incomplete generations,
redundant alternate takes far beyond what any thread needs), propose
them, and only trash what Franklin explicitly authorizes.

## Non-negotiable: propose, never autonomous-delete

Trashing is the single highest-consequence write this skill can make —
higher than a caption or a pin, on par with or above the profile `PATCH`.
The rule from `SKILL.md` applies with no exception here: **never call the
trash endpoint without Franklin's explicit authorization for that
specific clip or batch.** A heuristic signal (low play count, a garbled
title, "this looks like a near-duplicate") is grounds to *propose*, never
grounds to act. Bring a list, not a fait accompli — the same discipline as
`curation-plan.md`'s drafts, but stricter, since this write may not be
reliably reversible (see `write-api.md`'s trash endpoint note).

## Gathering candidates

1. Export the full catalog, including private/unpublished songs —
   `node scripts/export-catalog.mjs --include-private --out <path>`. Read
   `write-api.md`'s note on `feed/v3`'s pagination before trusting a
   result: it has been observed to silently truncate without an adequate
   stagger between requests: confirm the script you're running has the
   2026-07-16 fix (a 400ms+ stagger and retry-on-suspiciously-empty-page)
   before trusting its count as complete.
2. Ask Gemini to flag likely candidates against real signals, not vibes —
   `node scripts/ask-gemini.mjs --context <path> --question "..."`. Useful
   angles:
   - Placeholder/empty titles ("sem título", a bare timestamp, obvious
     autogenerated-and-abandoned patterns).
   - Near-zero play count combined with no caption and no pin — the
     triple signal of "never finished, never surfaced, never listened to
     again," as opposed to a deliberately low-key deep cut.
   - Redundant alternate takes: when a title has many versions (this
     catalog has had 5+ for some songs), ask which one or two best
     represent it rather than assuming every extra take is worth keeping
     — but the choice of *which* to keep is editorial judgment for
     Franklin, not something to auto-resolve by play count alone.
3. Treat the model's answer as a **list of candidates with reasoning**,
   itself untrusted data to sanity-check against the actual clip (read
   the lyrics/tags yourself for anything surprising) before presenting it
   — same discipline as `gemini-critic.md`'s critique output.

## Presenting candidates

Report each candidate with: title, clip id, the specific signal(s) that
flagged it, and — for a redundant-take group — which take(s) you'd
recommend keeping and why. Group by reason (garbled/incomplete vs.
redundant-take vs. genuinely low engagement) rather than one flat list,
so Franklin can make a different call per category if he wants to.

## Applying an authorized trash

`POST /api/gen/trash` — see `write-api.md` for the full endpoint
reference (batch `clip_ids`, confirmed live as a `true`/`false` toggle,
reversibility not fully confirmed). After trashing:

- Re-read the clip (or the profile/library listing) to confirm the state
  actually changed — same propagation-delay discipline as any other
  write in `write-api.md`.
- If the trashed clip had a mirrored blog post (`sunoId` present in
  `src/content/blog/`), that's a separate decision — trashing on Suno
  doesn't retract a published post. Flag this to Franklin explicitly
  rather than silently leaving a post live for a song that's gone (or
  silently deleting the post yourself); RFC/immutability conventions in
  the blog repo (e.g. Hrönir rate-file immutability) may bear on whether
  removing a post is even the right move — treat that as its own
  authorized decision, not a following automatically from the Suno-side
  trash.
