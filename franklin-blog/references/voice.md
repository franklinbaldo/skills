# Voice as authorship, not a preset

This reference is for diagnosing voice drift after a draft exists. It is not a recipe that
every post must instantiate.

The blog has recurring tendencies, but they are **evidence about an evolving author**, not a
style sheet. The safest question is not “does this sound short/dry/confessional enough?” It is:

> Does this draft preserve the way Franklin is thinking in this particular piece, or has the
> model replaced it with a familiar writerly solution?

## Stable signals

Across registers, several qualities recur often enough to be useful diagnostics.

### The author is present

Presence does not require first person. It means the text has selection, judgment, curiosity,
risk, or oddness specific enough that it could not have been generated by averaging competent
prose on the subject.

Sometimes presence is a confession. Sometimes it is an unexpectedly practical distinction, a
strange juxtaposition, a very specific annoyance, a technical obsession, a joke, a refusal to
resolve something, or the exact object chosen for attention.

### Epistemic posture is visible

The prose distinguishes what is known, inferred, suspected, remembered, or merely associated.
“I don't know” can remain unresolved. Strong claims can also remain strong when evidence earns
them. Do not make everything tentative in the name of authenticity.

### Thought moves

The reader should feel some change: a discovery, reversal, accumulation, collision, narrowing,
opening, or re-description. A perfectly polished summary with no movement often feels less
like a blog post and more like generated explanatory content.

### Lateral connections are permitted

A connection can illuminate without becoming a thesis. Juxtaposition sometimes does all the
work. Do not retrofit formal bridges merely to make the outline look rigorous.

### The reader is not abandoned

Technical, legal, philosophical, musical, or internet-native material may appear without
changing register into a textbook. Give the outside reader enough local context to keep
walking. Explanation is service, not a performance of expertise.

## Variable dimensions

These are **axes**, not targets. Calibrate each post independently.

### Sentence rhythm

Short, direct, cumulative sentences are common and often effective. They are not mandatory.
Long periods, fragments, lists, dialogue, or dense lyrical passages can be correct when the
thought demands them.

The smell is not “long sentence.” The smell is a sentence whose shape came from a remembered
house style rather than from the current thought.

### Confession

Confessional prose is one recurring register, not the definition of the author. Never invent a
memory, emotion, belief, or vulnerability to make a post feel personal.

A technical or documentary post may carry authorship entirely through selection and judgment.

### Humor

Dry humor and self-mockery often work well because they reduce pomposity and can carry an
argument. But jokes are optional. Do not insert one merely to counterbalance seriousness or
vulnerability. A passage can earn the right to remain bare.

### Closure

Some posts close tightly. Others stop with productive residue. Do not force either pattern.
Watch especially for generated conclusions that summarize what the reader just read and turn
an open thought into a packaged lesson.

### Structure

The blog legitimately contains compact fragments, long cumulative essays, practical pieces,
technical explanations, lyrical work, meme-native writing, and hybrids. Repetition of one
successful shape is a stronger warning sign than departure from it.

### Erudition and references

References are useful when they change the object, compress an explanation, or open a real
side path. They are harmful when they function mainly as credentials.

Do not imitate a named writer. The reference pool exists to widen possibility, not to provide
a target voice.

## Common model regressions

### Generic competence

The draft becomes smoother, safer, more complete, and less authored. Every sentence works.
None needs to be Franklin's.

Repair by returning to the strongest supplied phrases, judgments, objects, and tensions — not
by sprinkling quirks over generic prose.

### Manufactured intimacy

The model adds “I remember”, “I felt”, childhood scenes, embarrassment, fear, nostalgia, or
other first-person material not present in the source evidence.

This is fabrication, not voice work. Remove it.

### Defensive tightening

A loose association becomes a carefully hedged mini-thesis with three caveats. An admission is
followed by an unnecessary defense. The prose begins answering reviewers who are not there.

Restore the original epistemic scale.

### Style cosplay

Short sentences, deadpan lines, parentheticals, memes, Borges/Wittgenstein references, or a
cryptic ending recur because they are remembered features of the blog rather than because the
post needs them.

A trait can be authentic in one post and cosplay in the next.

### Explanatory takeover

Generosity to the reader expands until the post becomes a tutorial and the originating thought
disappears. Explain locally, then return to the thing the post is doing.

## A practical fidelity pass

After drafting, identify:

1. three sentences or moves that are strongly grounded in Franklin's supplied material;
2. three places that could plausibly appear in any competent article on the same topic;
3. any first-person claim whose source cannot be pointed to;
4. any recurring blog trick that appeared automatically rather than because this piece asked
   for it;
5. the post's current register on the variable dimensions above.

Rewrite the generic parts by recovering source-specific thought. Delete ungrounded personal
claims. Keep genuine variation.

The goal is not consistency of surface style. It is continuity of authorship.
